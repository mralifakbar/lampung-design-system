Alert

An inline message about this page, section or form, in one of four tones. The words carry the meaning; the tint and the icon only support them.

Usage

Use it when

  • Something on this page needs attention before the person acts: an overlapping schedule, a missing document, a declined payment.
  • A form failed on submit and you need one summary above it (field errors still sit under each field).
  • A result that stays relevant while the person is on the page: "Your request was sent. We reply within two working days."
  • A short explanation of a rule that changes what the person can do here ("Registration closes Friday at 17:00").

Use something else when

  • The condition is about the whole app, not this page (offline, maintenance, a trial that ends): use Banner.
  • Confirming something that just happened ("Saved", "Message sent"): use Toast. A toast leaves by itself; an alert stays until the condition is gone.
  • Asking the person to confirm a destructive action: use Alert dialog.
  • One field is wrong: use Text field. Put the error under the field (field__error), not in an alert.
  • A one-time tip about a gesture or a feature: use Hints.
  • A region has no data yet: use Empty state.

Where it goes. At the top of the region it is about: under the page header, above the form it summarizes, or at the top of a card. It takes the full width of its column on every screen size. Never floating, never stacked more than two high; if there are three conditions, merge them into one alert with a list.

Examples

Anatomy

PartWhat it is
.alertRequired. A flex row on the quiet fill with a 12 px gap and 14/16 px padding.
.alert--info|success|warning|dangerThe tone. Sets the tint and the icon color. Without a tone the alert sits on the neutral fill.
.alert > .icRequired. The tone icon, rendered filled automatically: info, check_circle, warning, error.
.alert__titleOne line that says what happened or what is true. Bold, 14 px.
.alert__bodyOptional second sentence: what it means or what to do.
.alert__actionsOptional row of small buttons (btn--sm or btn--md) under the text.
.alert__closeOptional close button: an icon-btn pulled to the right corner. Pair it with .collapse and data-lds-dismiss.
.collapseWrapper that lets the alert close (or appear) by collapsing its height on a spring. Holds one div around the alert.

Variants and sizes

Class or attributeWhen
alert--infoNeutral news and rules. Accent tint.
alert--successA result that stays relevant on this page. Use a toast for a passing confirmation.
alert--warningA problem the person can still fix, or a consequence they should know about.
alert--dangerSomething failed or was refused. Say what failed and what to do next.
(no tone)A neutral note on the quiet fill; rare.

States

StateHow
dismissed.collapse.is-closed on the wrapper (added by data-lds-dismiss): height to zero and opacity out, then lds:dismiss bubbles from the button.
appearingRender the wrapper as .collapse.is-closed, then remove is-closed on the next frame so it opens on the spring instead of jumping in.

Motion and microinteractions

  • Dismiss and appear: the .collapse wrapper animates grid-template-rows 1fr to 0fr on --spring-smooth over --d-smooth (560 ms) while opacity runs on --ease-standard over --d-standard (240 ms). The content below follows the height, so nothing jumps.
  • It is a transition, so re-opening mid-collapse reverses from where it is.
  • The alert itself has no entrance flourish: no slide, no shake. A danger alert that arrives after submit may use shake on the submit button, not on the alert.
  • Reduced motion: the collapse becomes a 120 ms linear change.

Accessibility

  • Use role="alert" only for a danger alert that appears after an action (it interrupts the screen reader). Use role="status" for info or success that appears later. An alert present on page load needs no role.
  • The icon is decoration (aria-hidden="true"); the title must state the tone in words ("Your card was declined", not "Error").
  • The close button is an icon button with aria-label="Dismiss". After it closes, move focus to the next logical element; a collapsed alert stays in the DOM, so remove it or set hidden on lds:dismiss once the 560 ms collapse ends, or its button stays reachable by Tab.
  • Never rely on the tint alone; text contrast stays at body level on every tint.

Do and don't

check_circleDo

  • Write the title as the fact ("Two courses overlap on Thursday") and the body as the next step.
  • Put the one fix inside the alert as a button.
  • Remove the alert as soon as the condition is fixed.

cancelDon't

  • Stack several alerts; merge them or fix the cause.
  • Use an alert to confirm a save; that is a toast.
  • Use role="alert" on content that was there when the page loaded.
  • Restyle an alert as a colored card or add a shadow.

Behavior and code

Plain HTML. Optional. A close button with data-lds-dismiss inside a .collapse adds is-closed to the closest .collapse and fires lds:dismiss (bubbles). Nothing else needs JS.

React. From lampung-design-system/react.

import { Alert } from 'lampung-design-system/react';

<Alert tone="warning" title="Two courses overlap on Thursday"
  actions={<button className="btn btn--secondary btn--sm">Resolve conflict</button>}>
  Pick one before you submit your plan.
</Alert>
// tone="danger" renders role="alert"; the icon follows the tone.
