Phone bars

The chrome of a phone screen: a nav bar at the top whose large title collapses into it on scroll, and a tab bar of three to five destinations at the bottom. Both are translucent so content scrolls visibly under them.

Usage

Use it when

  • A phone-first app or installed web app with three to five top-level destinations people switch between all day: Today, Schedule, Courses, Profile.
  • A phone screen that needs a title, a back button and one or two actions at the top.

Use something else when

  • A responsive web app that also runs on desktops: use App shell. The shell turns into a top bar and drawer on phones by itself.
  • A website with a few pages: use Top nav.
  • Sections inside one screen: use Segmented control. Put a segmented control under the large title.
  • More than five destinations: use App shell.

Where it goes. Inside a full-height phone screen (phone screen): .navbar absolute at the top over the scroller, .large-title as the first content in the scroller, .tabbar absolute at the bottom. Both bars pad themselves for the notch and home indicator in a real app; the docs frame .phone is only for showing them.

States

StateHow
.navbar.is-scrolledMaterial and hairline shown, inline title faded and risen in. Set by data-lds-navbar once the scroller passes 44 px.
.tabbar__item[aria-current="page"]Accent color and the filled glyph, which pops once.
:active (tab)The glyph sinks to 0.86 and springs back.

Motion and microinteractions

  • Nav bar: the material fades in over --d-fast (160 ms) linear; the inline title fades over --d-fast and rises 6 px over --d-standard on --ease-enter. Scrolling back reverses both.
  • Tab bar: pressing scales the glyph to 0.86 on --spring-snappy; the new current glyph plays lds-pop once over --d-snappy (360 ms).
  • When the destination changes, the new content can stagger in at 35 ms per row (list stagger); never slide whole screens sideways between tabs.
  • Reduced motion: pops and fades drop to 120 ms linear; reduced transparency swaps the blur for a solid material.

Accessibility

  • The tab bar is a <nav aria-label>; items are links (or buttons in a single-page app) with aria-current="page". Labels are always visible.
  • Icon-only bar buttons need aria-labels that name the destination ("Back to courses").
  • The large title is the screen's <h1>; the inline bar title is a visual repeat and can be aria-hidden.
  • Tab items are full-height, at least 44 px tall and a quarter of the width each.

Do and don't

check_circleDo

  • Keep three to five tabs with one-word labels and filled glyphs for the current one.
  • Return to the top of a tab when its current item is tapped again.

cancelDon't

  • Hide labels or show only icons in the tab bar.
  • Put actions (New, Compose) in the tab bar; use a floating action button.
  • Add a second blurred surface; the two bars are the only translucent ones.

Behavior and code

Plain HTML. data-lds-navbar="navbarId" on the scroller toggles .is-scrolled on that nav bar once scrollTop passes 44 px, and sets --collapse (0 to 1 over the first 40 px) on the scroller so the large title fades as it slides under the bar. The tab bar needs no script; move aria-current from your router. Bar heights follow --phone-top (status bar inset, default 54 px) and --phone-bottom (home indicator inset, default 30 px); .navbar-gap is a spacer with the bar's height. Pushed screens use .navbar--inline and .navbar__back (navigation stack).
