Route schematic

The stops of one trip on a line: the part already travelled in the accent, the vehicle where it is now, and the place and time under each stop. A diagram, not a map, so it stays honest and readable at any width.

Usage

Use it when

  • Tracking one shipment, delivery or trip through a few known stops: warehouse, hub, port, customer.
  • The detail of a transfer or a booking with a layover: where it left, where it is, what comes next.
  • A route people need at a glance beside the details, where a street map would add noise and loading time.

Use something else when

  • The history of one record with times and people, not places: use Timeline.
  • A fixed sequence of steps in a form: use Steps.
  • How far a value is toward a goal: use Target bar.

Where it goes. At the top of a shipment or trip detail, full width of its column. Two to six stops. Under about 104 px per stop it turns vertical, stops listed down the left with their words beside them.

Examples

Anatomy

PartWhat it is
.route[data-lds-route]Required. The fill box; drawRoute (or the hook) fills it from a config. role="group" named by label.
svg.route__svgGenerated, hidden from assistive tech. .route__track (dotted, the whole way), .route__done (accent, the travelled part), .route__dot per stop (.is-done, .is-next).
ol.route__stops > li.route__stopGenerated. One per stop: .route__name and .route__sub (time, code, status), plus the state in words for screen readers.
.route__vehicleGenerated while under way. A ringed disc with the mode icon on the current leg; a flight turns along its arc.

Variants and sizes

Class or attributeWhen
mode: "flight"Each leg is an arc and the plane turns along it.
mode: "road" | "sea" | "rail"Straight legs; the truck, ship or route icon.
positionWhere the vehicle is, counted in stops from 0 (1.4 is 40 % from the second stop to the third). The last index means arrived: no vehicle.
.is-verticalSet by the script when a stop gets under about 104 px; stops run down the left.

States

StateHow
under wayPassed dots filled, the next dot ringed in the accent and its name in accent text, the vehicle on the current leg.
arrivedEvery dot filled, no vehicle, the last stop says when it arrived.
delayed or on holdSay it in the stop words ("Held at customs since 14:20") and with a badge beside the route; the schematic keeps its colors.
loadingA .skel block of the same height in the fill box.

Motion and microinteractions

  • First view: the travelled legs draw in one after another over about 1.1 s in total, then the vehicle fades and settles from 0.6 on --spring-smooth. Dots fill as the line reaches them.
  • Calling drawRoute again (another shipment, a new position) draws the travelled part in again from the start.
  • Resizing relays the stops at once, without animation.
  • Reduced motion: drawn at once, the vehicle simply appears.

Accessibility

  • The box is a group named by label, which should say the trip and where it is in one sentence.
  • The stops are a real ordered list; each item ends with its state in words (Passed, Next stop, Ahead, Arrived).
  • The drawing and the vehicle are aria-hidden; nothing lives only in the picture.

Do and don't

check_circleDo

  • Write a time or status under every stop.
  • Keep it to the stops people care about; two to six.
  • Pair it with a timeline when the events between stops matter.

cancelDon't

  • Draw coastlines, countries or roads around it; it is a schematic.
  • Loop the vehicle along the line; it stands where the trip is.
  • Color stops by status alone; the words carry it.

Behavior and code

Plain HTML. data-lds-route with a JSON config is drawn by init(). From code: drawRoute(el, { label, mode, position, stops: [{ name, sub }] }) returns { config, update(next) }; it redraws on resize.
