Stat row

A row of key figures without cards: big tabular numbers with their labels underneath, divided by hairlines. Its columns follow the width of the content column, not the screen.

Usage

Use it when

  • Two to four figures that summarize a page or a section: credits taken, average grade, tasks due.
  • The top of a report or a dashboard section, before the details.
  • Results after an exam: score, rank, time used.

Use something else when

  • Figures that each need their own card, an icon or a change: use KPI card.
  • A figure with its trend over time: use Sparkline stat.
  • One score that needs a pass or fail judgment: use Progress ring.
  • Many label and value pairs about one thing: use Key-value.
  • Figures compared across many items: use Table.

Where it goes. At the top of a page section, full width of the content column, right under the section heading. In a narrow column (a phone, a side panel) it becomes two columns automatically, and an odd last figure spans both. From 600 px of container width it shows up to --n figures in one row.

Examples

Anatomy

PartWhat it is
.stat-row-wrapRequired. A size container; the stat row reads its width.
dl.stat-rowRequired. The grid with a hairline on top. Set style="--n: 3" for the number of figures per row (max 4).
.stat-row--fourAdd for four or more figures: smaller numbers and correct dividers.
.stat-row__itemRequired. One figure: a div holding the dt and dds.
dt.stat-row__labelRequired. The label. First in the DOM, shown under the figure.
dd.stat-row__valueRequired. The figure: 30 px narrow, 34 to 40 px wide, tabular.
dd.stat-row__subOptional context under the label ("Of 24 allowed").

Variants and sizes

Class or attributeWhen
--n: 2 | 3 | 4Figures per row from 600 px of container width.
stat-row--fourFour or more figures: smaller numbers, dividers per row of four.

States

StateHow
loadingA skeleton bar in each value; labels already shown.
no dataWrite "None yet" or a dash-free "Not available" in the value; keep the label.

Motion and microinteractions

  • None at rest. On first view the figures may fade up once with reveal on scroll (data-lds-reveal with --i per item) or count up with number count; never both.
  • A figure that changes while visible rolls with a rolling number on --spring-smooth.

Accessibility

  • It is a <dl>: each label is the term, the figure and the sub line are its values. The label comes first in the DOM, so screen readers read "Credits taken, 21, of 24 allowed" even though the figure is shown first.
  • Units belong in the value or the sub line, not only in a color or icon.

Do and don't

check_circleDo

  • Keep it to two to four figures that matter most.
  • Write context in the sub line ("Of 24 allowed", "Up 0.1").
  • Set --n to the number of figures.

cancelDon't

  • Wrap each figure in a card or add icons and colors.
  • Use it for more than four figures; split into sections or use a table.
  • Show a trend arrow without words.
