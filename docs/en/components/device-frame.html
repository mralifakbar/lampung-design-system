Device frame

A phone body around a .phone screen: rounded bezel, side buttons, the camera island and a status bar, scaled to fit the page. On a real phone the same screen drops the body and fills the display with .device--bleed.

Usage

Use it when

  • Showing a phone app inside a wide page: a product tour, an examples page, a design review, onboarding for a mobile app.
  • A preview whose screens push, pull and recede like an installed app, where the bezel tells people it is a phone.
  • The same preview on a phone, where the frame should disappear and the app should fill the screen.

Use something else when

  • A small framed screen next to text in documentation: use Phone screen. Use .phone.phone--inline without a body.
  • A responsive web app that runs on phones and desktops: use App shell.
  • A screenshot or photo of a device: use Thumbnail.

Where it goes. Centered in its own column, with nothing overlapping it. Give the column a definite width (for example a 414 px grid track): the frame scales to that width and to the window height, never above its real size. Below 600 px add .device--bleed and let it fill the viewport under a small app bar.

Examples

Anatomy

PartWhat it is
.device[data-lds-device]Required. The 414 by 868 body with a 12 px bezel and side buttons. data-lds-device scales it with CSS zoom to fit; data-fit-margin (default 48) is the vertical room to leave.
.phoneRequired. The 390 by 844 screen inside, clipped to a 48 px radius.
.phone__contentThe screen's content as one layer: nav bars, scrollers and tab bars. While a sheet is open it recedes like a card. Sheets, scrims and the toaster stay outside it.
.device__statusStatus bar: time, .device__signal (four <i>), the wifi icon and .device__battery (--level 0 to 1). aria-hidden.
.device__islandThe camera island. aria-hidden.
.home-indThe home indicator at the bottom.
[data-lds-push="id id"]On the .phone: the ids of the sheets that push the content back while open (space separated).

Variants and sizes

Class or attributeWhen
.deviceThe full body, scaled to fit with data-lds-device.
.device.device--bleedNo body: the screen fills its container, --phone-top and --phone-bottom come from env(safe-area-inset-*).

States

StateHow
.phone.is-pushedA listed sheet is open: .phone__content scales to 0.92 under the status bar, rounds its corners and dims; the backdrop turns black and the status bar light.

Motion and microinteractions

  • Recede: .phone__content scales and rounds on --spring-smooth over --d-smooth, the brightness eases on --ease-standard. Closing the sheet reverses it from where it is.
  • Scaling to fit is not animated: it follows the window size at once.
  • Reduced motion: the content stays in place and only dims, over 120 ms.

Accessibility

  • The status bar, island and home indicator are decoration: aria-hidden="true".
  • Put the frame in a landmark with a name (for example <main aria-label="Phone preview">) so people can skip to it.
  • The frame is shown with CSS zoom, so text inside keeps its real size relative to the screen and stays crisp; never shrink it below about half size, where body text drops under 8 px.

Do and don't

check_circleDo

  • Give the column a fixed width so the frame has something to fit.
  • Switch to .device--bleed on phones instead of showing a phone inside a phone.
  • Keep sheets, scrims and the toaster outside .phone__content so they do not recede with it.

cancelDon't

  • Put real content in the status bar; it is a picture of one.
  • Add a second shadow or a colored backdrop behind the frame.
  • Use it for screenshots; use a thumbnail.

Behavior and code

Plain HTML. data-lds-device on .device sets --device-scale (CSS zoom) from the parent width and the window height minus data-fit-margin, on load and on resize. fitDevice(el) runs it by hand after a layout change. data-lds-push takes several sheet ids separated by spaces. Drags inside the frame (sheets, swipe rows, slide to confirm, the navigation stack) divide by the zoom so they stay under the finger.
